AI at Work Three indices: use, jobs and training
Sources

Is AI changing American work yet? No single dataset or study can say. This page brings together public data, published research and the main ideas about how technology changes work, in three indices that update as new releases come out. Any one source is a glimpse through fog; read together, they show more.

  • Use (the bridge)where change could startHow much of the country's paid work involves tasks people already use AI for.
  • Jobs (the city)whether it has arrivedWhether pay, the mix of jobs and what job ads ask for have changed more than usual.
  • Training (the binoculars)how the next workforce adaptsWhat students choose to study, and whether young workers still get the jobs most exposed to AI.

Tasks people use Claude for make up 23% of US wages. Pay hasn't changed more than usual, and job ads turned away from tasks AI can do from 2020 to 2022, before ChatGPT, with no further drop at its release. And 42% of new degrees are in fields that lead to the jobs most exposed to AI.

A series that grows with every release

The page always shows the most recent data that exist, and every series grows longer with time. Each source gets updated when new releases appear, adding a new point to our series.

A natural next layer would be data on what firms spend on AI, such as the Ramp AI Index, since it measures adoption where firms actually pay.

The Census Bureau publishes firm adoption every two weeks; OpenAI Signals and the Current Population Survey come out monthly; the Anthropic Economic Index updates with each release; and pay, jobs, degrees and enrollment update once a year.

Use (the bridge)

Anthropic Economic Index, use of Claude by O*NET task, with the split between automation and augmentation, releases from September 2025 to the June 2026 monthly file. OpenAI Signals v2.0, work-related messages by work activity, June 2026, shown beside Claude on the skill-group card; each platform sees only its own users, so the two are never averaged. BTOS, the share of US firms using AI, Census Bureau, biweekly from November 2025 to September 2026, with its published standard errors. OEWS, wages and employment by occupation, BLS, May 2025.

Jobs (the city)

OEWS, wages and employment by occupation, BLS, May 2012 to May 2025. O*NET 30.3, tasks, ratings and skills, Department of Labor. NLx job ads, the tasks named in the ads of the National Labor Exchange, monthly from September 2015 to September 2025, in the aggregates of Meisenbacher, Nestorov and Norlander. Occupation crosswalks from the Census Bureau, NCES and BLS.

Training (the binoculars)

IPEDS completions by field and institution, NCES, 2023 to 2025. National Student Clearinghouse, undergraduate enrollment at four-year institutions by field family, fall 2019 to fall 2024. ACS 2024, field of degree against occupation, which shows where graduates of each field work. CPS basic monthly, Census Bureau, January 2022 to August 2026. Exposure throughout is the task measure of Eloundou, Manning, Mishkin and Rock (2024).

About the drawing

The drawing is the view from the lighthouse at Point Bonita, at the mouth of the Golden Gate, looking across to the bridge and the city and, through the coin-operated binoculars, south down the peninsula to the campuses. The weather is the latest observation at San Francisco International Airport (station KSFO), published by the National Weather Service: its description and visibility set the sky and the fog, its wind sets how fast and which way they drift, and rain falls when the station reports it. The sun and the moon are placed for the time in San Francisco from the standard formulas for their positions, and the moon is drawn at its phase. The boats, planes, trains, whales and gulls are invented.
